Tarp Pay on Flatbed Loads: When the Extra Charge Covers the Work

Field notes for owner-operators pulling flatbed and step deck, October 2026.

Most flatbed drivers can tell you the worst tarp job they ever had: wet lumber, a 25 mph crosswind, and a shipper who wanted the load covered "before the rain." What fewer drivers can tell you is whether that job actually paid for the hour and a half it took. Tarp pay is one of those line items that gets agreed on in ten seconds over the phone and then argued about for weeks.

What you are really being paid for

A tarp charge is not only about the tarp. It covers the time to throw, fold and strap it, the wear on a $600 to $900 lumber tarp, the extra weight you haul around every day, and the risk of a slip on a wet load. When a broker offers a flat $50 for a full lumber tarp, they are pricing the fabric, not your afternoon.

The ranges above are what we hear quoted on the phone, not a published index. Your region, the season and the commodity will move them.

Three questions before you agree to tarp

  1. Is tarp pay separate from the line haul? Get it written on the rate confirmation as its own line. "All-in" rates have a way of quietly absorbing it.
  2. Who supplies the tarps? Some shippers provide them. If you use your own, ask for the higher end of the range.
  3. Is there a re-tarp at a second stop? Multi-stop steel loads often mean untarping and tarping twice. That is two charges, not one.

Do the clock math, not just the dollar math

A 90-minute tarp job on an 11-hour driving day is time you cannot drive. If the load already pushes your on-duty window, add the tarp time to your plan before you accept. A rough hours-of-service planner helps you see whether the tarp work turns a same-day delivery into a reset.

When to walk away

Pass on a tarped load when the broker refuses to list tarp pay separately, when the commodity needs an 8-ft drop but the offer is a smoke-tarp price, or when the weather on the pickup day means you will be tarping in the dark. One cheap tarp job is annoying. A pattern of them is how a flatbed truck ends up running at a loss without the driver noticing.

If you would rather not fight this battle on every call, it helps to have people who will haggle the tarp charge before you ever load and get it on paper.
